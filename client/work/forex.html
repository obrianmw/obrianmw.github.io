The problem

Forex requests passed through too many hands to track

A foreign currency application moves from the branch inputter to an authoriser, Treasury and Exchange Control before it's funded and closed. Every hand-off was a chance for a document to go missing, a deadline to pass or a client to be left waiting without news.

I built one system that everyone works in. An application captured at a branch appears for the authoriser, Treasury and Exchange Control straight away, and documents uploaded at one PC open at any other, because they all live on the same central database.

How an application flows

Six steps, each with its own owner

  1. Capture · Inputter
  2. Authorise · Authoriser
  3. Allocate · Treasury
  4. Fund · Auto or client
  5. Process · Exchange Control
  6. Close · Reconcile

Handled automatically along the way: returns and rejections, the 16:00 funding cut-off and forfeiture, part payments, closing at the client's request, payment reversal, and blocking clients with unreconciled advance payments.

What it does

Built for the way a bank actually works

Four-eyes controls

Maker-checker, own-branch rules, invoice and per-diem limits, and access rights by role or user.

Smart allocation

Any currency with rate and cross rate; a funds check against the core-banking balance skips the wait when money is there.

Automatic communication

Every client contact emailed or texted at each step, with scheduled reminders and branch alerts that are never sent twice.

Reconciliation built in

Advance payments tracked for 90 days with documents, authorisation and Exchange Control sign-off.

Reports & audit

Work queues, period reports and a full per-record audit, exported to Excel, Word, PDF, CSV, HTML or print.

Operations ready

End-of-day, month and year routines, encrypted backups, and clearly badged UAT and LIVE environments.
